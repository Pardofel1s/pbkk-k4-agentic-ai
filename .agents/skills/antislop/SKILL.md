# Jejaksa anti-slop

Preserve the J Trace assets, Midnight–Teal palette, Blade/Livewire stack, and distinction between demo data and actual testing. Read the current frontend PRD and GSM in the adjacent `kebutuhan/frontend` folder when changing their contracts; the user's latest visual direction takes precedence.

- Write about targets, scope, tool activity, evidence, and review. Replace vague slogans and repeated three-part claims with concrete product actions. Keep the approved Jejaksa tagline.
- Give each section a purpose and a distinct composition. Use rules, whitespace, a workflow, or a document excerpt where another identical rounded card would add no information.
- Use the locally hosted Plus Jakarta Sans family with moderate weights, comfortable leading, and restrained tracking. Keep complete phrases together when space permits; do not force desktop line breaks onto mobile.
- Keep bright teal for actions and a small number of focal details. Avoid gradient headline fills, ornamental pills, fake trust marks, and oversized repeated closing CTAs.
- Present the landing's closing action as a compact bottom bar with a clear destination. Keep the footer useful and legible instead of adding another promotional section.
- Loop the product illustration while its viewport region is visible. Stop its clock offscreen or in a hidden tab, release timers on navigation, and show the completed static illustration for reduced motion. Do not add playback buttons unless requested again.
- Vary motion by meaning: rise for a new section, a small pop for a result, fade for a scene exit, and expansion for a disclosure. Limit concurrent motion; preserve reading order, keyboard focus, and stable layout bounds.
- Verify with actual wheel/touch input, keyboard disclosure interaction, desktop/mobile screenshots, and reduced-motion settings. Programmatic `scrollIntoView` alone does not prove that the page can be scrolled by a person.

Make narrowly scoped changes to the existing interface. Do not add animation packages, invent backend readiness, or replace the product's visual identity to satisfy a style preference.
